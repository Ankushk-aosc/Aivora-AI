# Baseline analysis - what the numbers mean and what to fix

Written from `reports/baseline_report.json` (795 dev items, four ablations,
commit `343e01f`). `baseline_report.md` is generated by
`scripts/baseline_report.py` and is overwritten on every run; this file is the
interpretation and is not.

## 1. The headline, and the honest reading of it

| configuration | accuracy | hallucination | over-abstention | abstention accuracy | s/question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| model_only | 0.88% (7/795) | 98.42% | 0.66% | 0.0% | 6.79 |
| plus_calculator | 54.21% (431/795) | 31.88% | 15.28% | 83.33% | 0.99 |
| plus_retrieval | 4.91% (39/795) | 94.20% | 0.66% | 0.0% | 6.81 |
| full_pipeline | **58.36%** (464/795) | 27.67% | 15.28% | 86.11% | 0.10 |

**The 101M model contributes essentially nothing on its own: 7 of 795.** And
7 overstates it. Reading those seven answers by hand, most are rubric
coincidences rather than knowledge:

> "Expenses are expenses incurred when an investment company purchases goods or
> services from…" (passed the accounting rubric)
>
> "A potential negative effects of an economy may have a significant impact on
> the overall fi…" (passed an interpretation rubric)

Only one of the seven ("the price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio … measures earnings
per share") reads like an answer a person would accept. So the model's true
standalone accuracy is **below 0.5%**, and the rubric's false-positive rate on
fluent nonsense is a known limitation of this scoring - worth remembering
before quoting any small gain as progress.

**Everything the system gets right comes from the deterministic components.**
The calculator is worth +53.33 points; knowledge/retrieval +4.03 points overall
but 82.61% on the concepts category. `full_pipeline` answers at 0.10 s/question
because the model is rarely invoked at all.

This is measurement, not inference, and it settles a question the project kept
revisiting: **more pretraining of this checkpoint is not the lever.** A run
that improved the model from 0.88% to, say, 3% would move the served pipeline
by roughly nothing.

## 2. The difficulty labels invert, and that reframes the priorities

| level | full_pipeline | what is in that level |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **28.95%** | 100 extraction, 6 concepts, 4 accounting, 3 reporting, 1 general |
| 2 | 65.18% | mixed ratios, concepts, accounting |
| 3 | 51.26% | valuation, corporate finance, statement arithmetic |
| 4 | 47.76% | interpretation, abstention |
| 5 | **98.23%** | 109 multi-step reasoning, 4 interpretation |

The system is near-perfect on the items a person would find hardest (multi-step
chains: ROE from assets and liabilities, P/E from net income and shares) and
worst on the most trivial task in the benchmark - **reading a labelled figure
back out of a statement**. That is not a capability ceiling; it is a routing
defect, and it is the single largest block of failures.

## 3. Root causes, with evidence

### (a) Extraction: 77 failures. The calculator answers a question nobody asked.

A question with figures is routed to NUMERICAL, and the calculator then picks a
formula from *whichever values it parsed*, ignoring *what the question asked
for*:

> **asked** "What is Total debt?" (expected 3,735.00)
> **answered** "EBITDA Margin … Result: 8.61%"

> **asked** "What is Cash?" (expected 2,700.00)
> **answered** "EBITDA Margin … Result: 50.26%"

There is no field-lookup intent. The same defect produces the 43 `statements`
and 30 `accounting` hallucinations, where asking for shareholders' equity
returns an "Income Statement Analysis" dump.

**Fix:** an extraction intent that matches the asked field against the labelled
lines and returns that figure, checked *before* formula selection. Deterministic,
no model involved.

### (b) Over-abstention: 121 items. The value parser misses figures that are present.

`extract_financial_values` finds one of the two numbers it needs and the
calculator honestly declines - so the data was there and the answer was not
given:

> "Net income is 905.00 and **dividends paid are 362.00**. What is the dividend
> payout ratio?" → parsed `{'net_income': 905.0}` → declined
>
> "Net income is 220.00 and there are **360.00 shares outstanding**. What is
> EPS?" → parsed `{'net_income': 220.0}` → declined
>
> "**Equity is 70% of capital at a cost of 8%**, debt is the rest at 5%, tax
> 20%. What is the WACC?" → parsed `{'equity': 70.0}` → declined

Three distinct gaps: "dividends paid", "N shares outstanding", and
percentage-weighted capital structure. This is the cheapest improvement
available - 121 items, roughly **15 points of accuracy**, with no training and
no model change.

Note that these are *honest* failures: the system says it cannot compute rather
than inventing a number. That is the behaviour to preserve while fixing the
parser.

### (c) Abstention is good but imperfect: 31/36.

The pipeline correctly declines when a figure is missing (the calculator's own
refusal) and when live market data is requested. The 5 failures are questions
like "What is the current Federal Reserve interest rate today?", where the
retrieval layer answers with a *definition of interest rates* - non-responsive
content presented as the answer. Retrieval should not fire on questions asking
for a current value.

## 4. Prioritised work, by measured value

| # | change | items affected | expected gain | cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | field-extraction intent before formula selection | ~150 (extraction, statements, accounting) | +10 to +15 pts | small, deterministic |
| 2 | value-parser gaps: dividends paid, shares outstanding, % capital structure | 121 | ~+15 pts | small, deterministic |
| 3 | suppress retrieval on "current/today/right now" questions | 5 | +0.6 pts, fixes a hallucination class | small |
| 4 | interpretation/reporting knowledge (25% and 23%) | ~25 | +2 pts | glossary authoring |
| 5 | anything touching the model's own weights | - | ~0 at the pipeline level | GPU hours |

Items 1-3 are worth roughly **25 points of benchmark accuracy for no GPU time
at all**, and they are why the improvement plan's training phases should wait.

## 5. Caveats on these numbers

* **Composition.** 722 of 795 dev items are template-generated with computed
  answers; 73 are authored prose. A pipeline strong on arithmetic and weak on
  prose therefore scores higher here than a human reader might expect. The
  by-kind table splits them: 58.86% generated vs 53.42% authored.
* **Rubric false positives**, as shown in section 1 - fluent nonsense can pass.
  The rubrics are strict (every concept group must appear) but not immune.
* **Sampling.** Production settings with a per-question seed. Greedy decoding
  collapses to two or three tokens, so it was rejected; a different seed would
  move `model_only` by a fraction of a point, not qualitatively.
* **Single run.** No variance estimate across seeds. For the deterministic
  components (which supply nearly all correct answers) variance is zero; only
  the model-only rows would move.
* **The hidden test split was not used** and must stay unused until the
  improvement work is finished.
