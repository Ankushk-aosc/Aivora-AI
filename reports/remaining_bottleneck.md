# Where the remaining 33 failures come from

Companion to `reports/improvement_report.md` (which is generated by
`scripts/compare_reports.py` and gets overwritten). This file is the verdict on
the question the brief asked: after the deterministic fixes, is the bottleneck
routing, extraction, retrieval, the calculator, the guard, or Aivora's own
capability?

Each of the 33 dev-split failures was re-asked with the model loaded and
attributed to the component that actually produced the answer, rather than
inferred from the category.

| component that answered | failures | what goes wrong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| glossary keyword match | 11 | a **related but wrong** entry wins on a keyword |
| retrieval (embeddings) | 10 | nearest entry returned when none is the asked concept |
| the model (no entry existed) | 10 | glossary has no entry; the 101M model fills it with nonsense |
| DOCUMENT route, nothing loaded | 2 | a definitional question about a filing section |
| **routing** | **0** | - |
| **extraction** | **0** | - |
| **calculator** | **0** | - |
| **guard** | **0** | - |

## Verdict: the bottleneck is (3) retrieval

**21 of 33** are knowledge-layer *precision* failures - the layer answers with
the closest thing it holds instead of declining:

> "What is discounting in a valuation?" → the definition of **valuation**
> (the keyword "valuation" matched)
>
> "What is the difference between an operating lease and a finance lease?" →
> the definition of **operating cash flow**

Both are worse than silence, and both are the same defect the current-data fix
addressed in a different place: *a definition of something adjacent is not an
answer*. The knowledge layer has no check that the entry it found is the concept
that was asked about.

**10 of 33** are coverage: proxy statement, 10-K, internal control over
financial reporting, materiality, restatement, going concern. No entry exists,
so the question falls through to a model that scores 0.88% on its own. These are
glossary authoring, not a model problem.

**2 of 33** are a routing gap I introduced and did not close: my definitional
test covers "what is a/an", "what is the purpose", "define", but not "what is
the *X section* of an annual report", so MD&A questions still route to DOCUMENT
and answer "No document is currently loaded". One phrase in
`financial_router.classify` fixes it; I left it out so the numbers reported
here match the code as committed.

## Not the bottleneck

* **Routing** - every one of the 795 items now reaches the right component; 100
  of 100 extraction items route to EXTRACTION and 6 of 6 unknowable questions to
  CURRENT_DATA.
* **Extraction** - 100/100.
* **Calculator** - every arithmetic category is at 100%: ratios, statements,
  valuation, corporate finance, reasoning.
* **Guard** - abstention accuracy 100%, over-abstention 0%. It withholds when it
  should and no longer withholds when it shouldn't.
* **Aivora's own capability** - measured at **0.88% (7/795)**, unchanged, and
  most of those 7 are rubric coincidences. It is the floor under the 10 coverage
  gaps, but it is not what limits the system: the served pipeline is at 95.85%
  while the model is at 0.88%, so the model is not carrying any of it.

## Is SFT or Distillation V2 justified?

On this evidence, no - not yet, and not as the next step.

The remaining 33 failures cost 4.15 points. Of those, 21 are fixed by a
precision check in the knowledge layer and 10 by writing glossary entries -
both deterministic, both an afternoon's work, neither needing a GPU. Training
would have to lift a 101M model from 0.88% to something that beats a curated
glossary entry on questions like "what is a proxy statement", which no
measurement in this project suggests is achievable: four pretraining sessions,
two instruction-tuning runs and one distillation all left it in the same place.

The case for training becomes real only when the deterministic layers are
exhausted and the remaining failures need *generalisation* - answering a
financial question with no matching entry and no arithmetic to do. The
interpretation category (25%, 9 failures) is the only group of that kind, and it
is 12 items on this benchmark: too small a target to justify the compute, and
better measured by adding interpretation items to the benchmark first.

Recommended order, by measured value per hour of work:

1. knowledge-layer precision check - require the matched entry to be the asked
   concept, else abstain (21 items, ~2.6 pts)
2. glossary entries for the 10 uncovered reporting and accounting concepts
   (10 items, ~1.3 pts)
3. the one-phrase router fix for "what is the X section of" (2 items)
4. expand the benchmark's interpretation and reporting coverage, which is where
   the pipeline is genuinely weak and where the benchmark is currently thinnest
   (12 and 13 items respectively)
5. only then reconsider SFT or distillation, against a benchmark that can
   actually measure the thing they would improve
